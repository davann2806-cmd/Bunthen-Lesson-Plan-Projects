### 1.2 Erratum #1 — CRITICAL Theorem Box Reversal & Omitted Converse Proof (`Book p. 158`)
- **Verbatim Bullet 1**: `• បើជ្រុងពីរមិនប៉ុនគ្នា មុំដែលឈមនឹងជ្រុងទាំងពីរនេះក៏មិនប៉ុនគ្នាដែរ ហើយមុំដែលឈមនឹងជ្រុងធំជាង ជាមុំធំជាង` *(omits closing `។`; restore `។`)*.
- **Verbatim Bullet 2 (ERRATUM)**: `• ច្រាសមកវិញ បើមុំពីរមិនប៉ុនគ្នាជ្រុងដែលឈមនឹងមុំទាំងពីរនេះក៏មានប៉ុនគ្នាដែរ ហើយជ្រុងដែលឈមនឹងមុំធំជាងជាជ្រុងធំជាង ។`
  - **CRITICAL FACTUAL CONTRADICTION**: Textbook erroneously prints **`ក៏មានប៉ុនគ្នាដែរ`** (*"the sides opposite those two angles are ALSO equal"*) instead of **`ក៏មិនប៉ុនគ្នាដែរ`** (*"the sides opposite those two angles are ALSO not equal"*)!
  - **Mandatory E3 Fix**: Print **`ក៏មិនប៉ុនគ្នាដែរ`** cleanly in all Wave 2/3 theorem boxes, restore closing `។` on Bullet 1, and explicitly flag this textbook misprint in `Teaching_Guideline_INEQ1.tex` so teachers guide students to correct it in their books.
- **Required 3-Case Trichotomy / Contradiction Proof of the Converse ($\angle B > \angle C \implies AC > AB$)**:
  - Textbook states the converse without proof. E3 must supply the rigorous 3-case **trichotomy (`លក្ខណៈបីករណី`) / proof by contradiction (`Baccaglini-Frank et al., 2013`)**:
    For segments $AC$ and $AB$ in $\triangle ABC$ with given $\angle B > \angle C$, exactly one of three order relations holds: **(Case 1)** $AC = AB$, **(Case 2)** $AC < AB$, or **(Case 3)** $AC > AB$.
    - *Suppose Case 1 ($AC = AB$)*: By the Isosceles Base Angles Theorem (Slice 5), $\angle B = \angle C$, contradicting given $\angle B > \angle C$.
    - *Suppose Case 2 ($AC < AB$)*: By the Direct Side-Angle Inequality Theorem ($AB > AC$), $\angle C > \angle B$, contradicting given $\angle B > \angle C$.
    - *Conclusion*: Both Case 1 and Case 2 produce impossible triangles (`pseudo objects`), leaving **Case 3 ($AC > AB$)** true.

### 1.3 Erratum #2 — `លំហាត់គំរូ` Degree-Symbol Omission (`Book pp. 158–159`)
- **Verbatim Prompt (`p. 158`)**: `ក្នុង ΔABC មាន ∠A = 6x + 4° , ∠B = 7x + 12° និង ∠C = 6x − 7° ។ ចូររៀបជ្រុងនៃ ΔABC តាមលំដាប់ពីធំទៅតូច ។`
- **Verbatim Solution (`p. 159`) & Typo**:
  - `∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°` $\implies (6x + 4^\circ) + (7x + 12^\circ) + (6x - 7^\circ) = 180^\circ \implies 19x + 9^\circ = 180^\circ$.
  - **Line 4 Erratum**: Textbook prints **`19x = 180° − 9 = 171°`** (omits `°` on `9`!). **Fix to**: `19x = 180° - 9° = 171°`.
  - Solving gives $x = \frac{171^\circ}{19} = 9^\circ \implies \angle A = 6(9^\circ)+4^\circ = 58^\circ$, $\angle B = 7(9^\circ)+12^\circ = 75^\circ$, $\angle C = 6(9^\circ)-7^\circ = 47^\circ$ (check: $58^\circ + 75^\circ + 47^\circ = 180^\circ$).
  - **Mandatory Pedagogical Enhancements**: Textbook includes *no diagram* and jumps straight from angle measures to $AC > BC > AB$. Wave 2/3 must render a scalene triangle diagram labeled $58^\circ, 75^\circ, 47^\circ$ AND enforce a 3-row **Vertex-to-Opposite-Side Mapping Table** ($\angle B = 75^\circ \leftrightarrow AC$, $\angle A = 58^\circ \leftrightarrow BC$, $\angle C = 47^\circ \leftrightarrow AB \implies AC > BC > AB$), plus distinguish variable $x$ from vertex labels (`Ataş-Koçluk & Tanışlı, 2026`).

### 1.4 Erratum #3 — `ប្រតិបត្តិ` Geometric Construction & Exact Coordinates (`Book p. 159`)
- **Verbatim Prompt**: `ប្រតិបត្តិ : ក្នុងរូបខាងស្ដាំនេះគេឱ្យ AB = AC = BD និង ∠ABC < ∠BAC បង្ហាញថា BD < BC ។` *(Textbook omits `។` after `∠BAC`; restore `។`.)*

In the PDF scan (`crop_pratebat.png`), $B$ leans left of $A$ ($\angle BAC \approx 113.7^\circ$), whereas any sketch/mental image with acute $\angle BAC < 90^\circ$ is geometrically impossible under $AB = BD$ (because $\angle BAD = 180^\circ - \angle BAC > 90^\circ$ forces hypotenuse-like opposite side $BD > AB$, making $BD$ appear roughly `1.4x` longer than $AB$!).
- **Mandatory Exact Euclidean Obtuse $\angle BAC = 108^\circ$ Coordinates (`x=1cm, y=1cm` Scaled by $R$)**:
  - Place collinear $D\text{-}A\text{-}C$ with exact coordinates:
    $$A = (0,\, 0), \qquad C = (R,\, 0), \qquad B = (-0.3090\,R,\, 0.9511\,R), \qquad D = (-0.6180\,R,\, 0)$$
    *(where $\cos 108^\circ = -0.309017$, $\sin 108^\circ = 0.951057$, $2\cos 108^\circ = -0.618034$).*
  - **Verification**: $AB = AC = BD = R$ identically (true to `0.01%`), $\angle BAC = 108^\circ > \angle ABC = 36^\circ$ ($\angle ACB = 36^\circ$), $\angle BAD = 72^\circ = \angle BDA$, and $BC = 2R\sin 54^\circ = 1.6180\,R > R = BD$.
  - **Proof**: In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle ABC < \angle BAC \implies AC < BC$ (Converse Side-Angle Inequality). Since $AB = AC = BD$, substituting $BD$ for $AC$ directly yields $BD < BC$. *(Explicitly warn learners not to get trapped trying to deduce $BD < BC$ inside $\triangle ABD$ alone without using $BD = AC$.)*

- **Ex. `#14` (Verbatim Prompt)**: `14. ចំពោះត្រីកោណនីមួយៗខាងក្រោមសរសេរជ្រុងតាមលំដាប់ពីវែងទៅខ្លី` *(add `។`)*:

- **Ex. `#15` (Verbatim Prompt & Markings)**: `15. PQR ជាត្រីកោណមួយដែល QR > QP និង PR = PQ ។ បង្ហាញថា ∠P > ∠Q ។`

  - *Proof*: In $\triangle PQR$, $QR > QP \implies \angle P > \angle R$ (Direct Side-Angle Inequality). Since $PR = PQ$, $\triangle PQR$ is isosceles at apex $P$, so $\angle Q = \angle R$ (Isosceles Base Angles Theorem). Substituting $\angle R = \angle Q$ yields $\angle P > \angle Q$.

## 3. Page-Grounded Empirical & Didactical Synthesis (`Targets a–d`)

### (a) Side-Angle Inequality, Auxiliary-Point ($AD=AB$) Construction & Exterior-Angle Reasoning
- **The Auxiliary-Line Operative Bottleneck (`Gridos et al., 2019`; `Winer & Battista, 2022`; `Biber, 2020`)**:
  - `Gridos et al. (2019)` tested `N = 149` students on geometry multiple-solution tasks with and without figures. While accompanying diagrams significantly increased fluency (`M = 2.16, SD = 1.42` vs. `M = 1.44, SD = 1.21`, `t = 6.02, df = 146, p < .001`) and flexibility (`M = 19.88` vs. `13.93`, `t = 5.57, p < .001`), Problem 2 revealed a striking gap between passive angle chasing and **operative apprehension** (mereologic modification via auxiliary lines): **`28.4%`** solved Problem 2 without auxiliary construction (`Solutions 2a/2b`), whereas only **`2.7%`** (`Solution 1b`, drawing median $DB$) and **`1.4%`** (`Solution 3`, drawing parallel $DM$) succeeded when an **auxiliary line** was required, and **`41.5%`** gave zero solutions (`PDF pp. 4–6`).
  - `Winer & Battista (2022, p. 13)` found that in **`6/14`** student proofs requiring auxiliary segments, learners drew the line on the diagram and merely wrote `"Construction"` without deducing why the point/segment exists in the axiomatic system. For Slice 6's `ឧទាហរណ៍` (`Book p. 158`), students must connect `AD = AB` to **`AD = AB < AC`** (betweenness warrant forcing $D$ strictly between $A$ and $C$, so $BD$ splits $\angle ABC = \angle B_1 + \angle B_2$).
  - Once auxiliary segment $BD$ is drawn, $\triangle ABD$ overlaps with $\triangle ABC$ and shares vertex/ray structure with $\triangle BCD$; `Biber (2020, Table 2, p. 1151)` documents that overlapped triangle configurations drop accuracy to **`44%–67%`** vs. **`71%–95%`** for adjacent triangles, motivating separate `[tracing]` sub-steps (`Yeo & Tzeng, 2019`) and `Looking Back` (`Miyazaki et al., 2019, pp. 6–7` on lower-secondary exterior-angle proofs).

### (b) Illusion of Linearity / False Proportionality ($\angle B = 2\angle C \not\Rightarrow AC = 2AB$)
- **Empirical Evidence (`De Bock et al., 2003`; `Ataş-Koçluk & Tanışlı, 2026`)**:
  - `De Bock et al. (2003)` tested `N = 313` students (`152` Grade 8 students aged 13–14; `161` Grade 10 students aged 15–16) and replicated on `N = 165`. Students scored `95%` on proportional items vs. **`33%`** overall on non-proportional geometry items (`F(1,302) = 430.64, p < .01`), with **Grade 8 students scoring only `23%`** vs. `43%` in Grade 10 (`F(1,302) = 13.81, p < .01`), and `95%` of incorrect answers traced to inappropriate proportional reasoning (`Footnote 2, p. 451`).
  - Crucially, `De Bock et al. (2003)` found that asking students to draw a scaled copy **worsened** non-proportional accuracy (`23%` Drawing vs. `44%` No-Drawing, `F(1,302) = 10.38, p < .01`; replication `40%` vs. `62%`, `F(1,159) = 22.95, p < .01`), because measuring and scaling linear elements primed the linear factor in working memory (`p. 460`).
  - In Slice 6 (`៣.១`), learners readily over-extend *monotonic order* ($AC > AB \Leftrightarrow \angle B > \angle C$) into *linear proportionality* ($\angle B = 2\angle C \Rightarrow AC = 2AB$!). `Ataş-Koçluk & Tanışlı (2026, pp. 14, 22)` independently confirm Grade 8 students' linear overgeneralization in polygon/exterior-angle tasks (`misconception of linearity`, producing `2n`). **Pedagogical Countermeasure**: Immediately confront the `30°-60°-90°` (`#14(1)`!) and `45°-45°-90°` triangles in E2/E3: $\angle B = 60^\circ = 2 \times 30^\circ = 2\angle C$, yet $AC = \sqrt{3}\,AB \approx 1.73\,AB \neq 2\,AB$ (and in a `90°-45°-45°` triangle, doubling the `45°` angle to `90°` only increases the side by $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41 \neq 2$)!

### (c) Opposite-Element Mapping & Algebraic-to-Geometric Triangle Ordering
- **Evidence (`Ataş-Koçluk & Tanışlı, 2026`; `Biber et al., 2013`; `Altıparmak & Gürcan, 2021`; `Scristia et al., 2022`; `Xu et al., 2026`)**:
  - `Ataş-Koçluk & Tanışlı (2026)` screened `N = 187` Grade 8 students (`24` at Van Hiele analytic level; `6` clinical interviews) and documented: **(1)** uppercase/lowercase confusion treating vertex labels (`A`) vs. algebraic unknowns (`x`) inconsistently (`p. 12`), **(2)** incomplete equation modeling omitting the `180°` sum relationship (`p. 13`, e.g., $4x=180$), and **(3)** procedural solving that only succeeds conceptually when students validate numeric outputs against geometric triangle constraints (`p. 13`).
  - `Biber et al. (2013)` analyzed `N = 30` **Grade 8** students across 4 open-ended angle problems (`43%` Q1, `37%` Q2, `50%` Q3, `43%` Q4 incorrect), identifying two Van Hiele visualization/analysis barriers (`pp. 58–59`): **(i)** judging properties by visual appearance alone (assuming perpendicularity or summing arbitrary given numbers), and **(ii)** detecting isolated properties (e.g., `180°` or `360°`) without linking them into a unified reasoning chain—mirroring `Xu et al. (2026)` (`p < .001`) on fragmented vs. hub-and-spoke knowledge and `Scristia et al. (2022, pp. 3163–3164)` on missing deductive warrants (`AY`).
  - `Altıparmak & Gürcan (2021, Table 15, p. 317)` and `Catman-Aksoy & Isiksal-Bostan (2024, p. 243)` show how orientation fixation and weak vertex-to-opposite-side reading cause students to misidentify which side faces $\angle B$ vs. $\angle A$ in `លំហាត់គំរូ` (`58°, 75°, 47°`) and `#13–#15`. Enforcing an explicit **3-row Opposite Mapping Column (`∠B ↔ AC`, `∠A ↔ BC`, `∠C ↔ AB`)** eliminates guessing!

### (d) Misleading Textbook Diagrams, Orientation Bias & Proof by Contradiction / Trichotomy
- **Evidence (`Dietiker et al., 2017`; `Baccaglini-Frank et al., 2013`; `Catman-Aksoy & Isiksal-Bostan, 2024`)**:
  - `Dietiker et al. (2017)` compared elementary (`n = 61`) vs. secondary (`n = 156`) textbook diagrams: elementary tasks reward treating figures *"as drawn with assumptions"* (`59.0%` vs. `22.4%`, `p < .001`), whereas secondary geometry demands **interpreting markings** (`68.6%` vs. `0%`, `p < .001`), **mentally manipulating/redrawing figures** (`10.3%` vs. `0%`, `p = .007`), and **not trusting unscaled appearances**. Strikingly, `Dietiker et al. (2017, p. 139)` specifically classify diagrams whose triangle angle measures **do not sum to `180°`** as textbook **"misrepresentations"**—matching **Erratum #4 (`#14(3)` `40°+80°+50°=170°`)** and **Ex. `#13(2)` (`1.1, 2, 3` drawn acute instead of `~149.5°` obtuse)**!
  - `Baccaglini-Frank et al. (2013)` show through the **`pseudo object` / `proto-pseudo object`** lens (`pp. 65–72`) why proof by contradiction is cognitively fragile: learners either fail to notice when contradictory assumptions yield an impossible figure (e.g., `Excerpt 3`, ignoring `91°` vs. `90°`), or gain sudden clarity once the contradictory case visually degenerates (`Excerpt 2`, parallel sides leaving *"a hole"* in a triangle). Visualizing Case 1 ($AC=AB$) and Case 2 ($AC<AB$) alongside the `108°` authentic `ប្រតិបត្តិ` figure turns trichotomy into concrete deduction!
