5. **Homework `#2`, `#8`, `#11` (`Book pp. 164–165`) Exact Givens, Tick Marks & Proof Blueprints**:

Given $PQ = PR$ and $\angle PST = \angle PTS$; compare $\triangle PQS$ and $\triangle PRT$.
     - **Critical `SSA` (`ជ.ជ.ម`) Trap**: Students using Converse in $\triangle PST \Rightarrow PS = PT$, supplementary angles $\angle PSQ = 180^\circ - \angle PST = 180^\circ - \angle PTS = \angle PTR$, and $PQ = PR$ arrive at **`SSA` (`ជ.ជ.ម`)**, which is **not** a valid congruence criterion!
     - **Valid Solution A (`180°` Angle Sum $\Rightarrow$ `ម.ជ.ម / ASA` or `ជ.ម.ជ / SAS`)**: From $PQ = PR \Rightarrow \angle Q = \angle R$; from $\angle PSQ = \angle PTR$ and triangle angle sum $180^\circ$, $\angle QPS = 180^\circ - \angle Q - \angle PSQ = 180^\circ - \angle R - \angle PTR = \angle RPT$. Hence $\triangle PQS \cong \triangle PRT$ by **`ម.ជ.ម (ASA)`** on $PQ = PR$ (or **`ជ.ម.ជ (SAS)`** with $PS = PT$).

   - **Exercise `#8` (`Book p. 165`)**: Triangle $ABC$ drawn upright (`AB = AC`), $M \in AB$ and $N \in AC$ midpoints, segments $BN$ and $CM$ intersecting at $P$. **Diagram has ZERO tick marks and ZERO angle arcs** (authors must teach students to transfer text givens onto the figure). Prove $\triangle PBC$ is isosceles: $BM = \frac{1}{2}AB = \frac{1}{2}AC = CN$ (from midpoints + $AB = AC$), $\angle MBC = \angle NCB$ (base angles of isosceles $\triangle ABC$), $BC = CB$ (common) $\Rightarrow \triangle MBC \cong \triangle NCB$ (**`ជ.ម.ជ / SAS`**) $\Rightarrow \angle PBC = \angle PCB \Rightarrow PB = PC$ (**Converse of Isosceles**) $\Rightarrow \triangle PBC$ is isosceles at apex $P$. *(Alternative path: $\triangle ABN \cong \triangle ACM$ by `SAS`, then angle subtraction $\angle ABC - \angle ABN = \angle ACB - \angle ACM$.)*
   - **Exercise `#11` (`Book p. 165`)**: Horizontal line $O–P–E–B$ in collinear order; $H$ above $O$ ($HO \perp OB$), $D$ above $B$ ($DB \perp OB$), segments $HE$ and $DP$ intersecting at $F$. **Diagram marks ONLY `90°` squares at $O$ and $B$** (no side ticks or arcs). Given $HO \perp OB$, $DB \perp OB$, $OP = BE$, $HO = DB$.

## 3. Page-Grounded Empirical & Didactical Synthesis

### (a) Isosceles Properties, Paper-Folding Symmetry, 4-in-1 Coincidence & Wrong-Apex / Converse Misconceptions
- **Paper-Folding Enactive Mechanism (`Olson, 1975`; `Coad, 2006`; `Catman-Aksoy & Isiksal-Bostan, 2024`)**:
  - `Olson (1975)` establishes two complementary folding constructions directly adaptable to Khmer printable paper strips: **(i)** *Activity 14* (`p. 8 / PDF p. 14`) folds an isosceles triangle along the perpendicular bisector/altitude through its apex so the base vertices coincide, grounding Base Angles congruence (`∠B = ∠C`) and reflectional symmetry in physical experience; **(ii)** *Activity 42* (`p. 24 / PDF p. 30`) folds the perpendicular bisector of segment $AB$ and connects any crease point $P$ to $A$ and $B$ to show why $\triangle ABP$ is always isosceles; and **Appendix B Theorems 1–3** (`p. 47 / PDF p. 53`) formalize the 4-in-1 coincidence along the apex symmetry axis.
  - `Coad (2006, pp. 6–8)` documents that folding with translucent paper makes angle/segment superposition plausible before introducing Euclidean rigor, while lowering affective resistance.
  - `Catman-Aksoy & Isiksal-Bostan (2024, pp. 242–245)` show with Grade 6 students that non-prototype orientations during paper folding raised perpendicularity/parallelism performance from Pre `M = 16.02 (SD = 10.04)` to Post `M = 31.47 (SD = 10.29)` out of 54, countering horizontal-base prototype fixation.
- **Definition–Drawing & Partitional Traps (`Altıparmak & Gürcan, 2021`)**:
  - Among `N = 156` students (`pp. 315–318`), `56` gave a correct verbal definition of an isosceles triangle, yet only **`23`** drew it accurately with clear equal-edge marking (`Table 14, p. 317`), while **`151/156`** failed to classify an equilateral triangle as a special case of an isosceles triangle (`Table 15, p. 317`; only `5/156` gave hierarchical inclusion). Lesson 5 must therefore enforce explicit tick-mark transfer (`|`) and non-horizontal apex orientation (tilted isosceles examples where apex is *not* at the top).
- **Theorem vs. Definition vs. Converse Slippage (`Winer & Battista, 2022`)**:
  - In Problem N (`pp. 7–8, 15`), students who correctly saw equal base angles during oral planning still introduced formalization gaps (`FG2`) in two-column proofs—either citing the *definition* of an isosceles triangle (`27/65` FG2 instances across tasks) when the Base Angles Theorem was required, or citing the *Base Angles Theorem* instead of its **Converse** (*Jenny*, `p. 8, 15`). Distinguishing **`ទ្រឹស្តីបទផ្ទាល់` ($AB=AC \Rightarrow \angle B=\angle C$)** from **`ទ្រឹស្តីបទច្រាស` ($\angle B=\angle C \Rightarrow AB=AC$)** in two parallel E3 boxes directly addresses this failure mode.

### (b) Circumcenter (`ប្រសព្វមេដ្យាទ័រ`), Obtuse Exterior Location & Four-Center Discrimination
- **Acute vs. Right vs. Obtuse Circumcenter Crisis (`Yıldız, Güven, & Koparan, 2010`)**:
  - Among `N = 25` **Grade 8** students (`Tables 3–4, p. 2043 / PDF p. 4`), pre-test success at **drawing perpendicular bisectors** dropped sharply from **Acute `60%` (`15/25`)** and **Right `76%` (`19/25`)** to **Obtuse `8%` (`2/25`)** (post: `68%`, `92%`, `32%`). Even more critically, pre-test success at **finding the circumcenter** ("center of perpendicular bisector") was **`8%` (`2/25`) for Acute**, **`0%` (`0/25`) for Right**, and **`0%` (`0/25`) for Obtuse** (`25/25` wrong pre; post: `16%`, `24%`, `12%`).
  - **Direct Instructional Mandate**: Do NOT teach circumcenter solely on an upright acute triangle! Confront Grade 8 students with an **obtuse triangle (`115°` or `110°`)** where the circumcenter $O$ moves **outside** the triangle (`ប្រសព្វមេដ្យាទ័រស្ថិតនៅខាងក្រៅត្រីកោណមុំទាល`)—plus note the special case on a right triangle (`O` at midpoint of hypotenuse) as extension.
- **Hub-and-Spoke Organization Against Four-Center Fragmentation (`Xu et al., 2026`; `Demiray et al., 2023`)**:
  - `Demiray, Işıksal-Bostan, & Saygı (2023)` document two exact classroom phenomena in circumcenter construction (`pp. 8, 11, 15–16`): **(i)** peers spontaneously challenge the acute assumption (*"what happens to the circumcenter when the given is an obtuse triangle?"*, `p. 8`), and **(ii)** learners conflate the **centroid (`ប្រសព្វមេដ្យាន`)** (`2k : k` ratio) with the **circumcenter (`ប្រសព្វមេដ្យាទ័រ`)** (`p. 11, 16`), or assume all centers coincide as in an equilateral triangle (`p. 15`).
  - `Xu et al. (2026)` compared fragmented linear instruction of the four triangle centers (Class A, `n = 61`) against a refined **hub-and-spoke** structure linking centroid, circumcenter, incenter, and orthocenter around explicit geometric rationales and worked examples (Class B, `n = 60`). Class B improved significantly on all diagnostic indicators (`all p < 0.001`, `Tables 1 & 3, pp. 9, 12–13`): clarity (`2.57 → 3.88`), approach acceptance (`2.39 → 3.50`), and integrated understanding/application (`2.56 → 4.32`, with `86.7%` selecting `4–5`), reducing rote memorization burden (`p. 9, 13`).
  - **Equidistant Locus Foundation (`Milin Šipuš et al., 2022`; `Bento et al., 2018`; `Olson, 1975`)**: `Milin Šipuš et al. (2022, pp. 103–104)` frame the perpendicular bisector as the boundary line equidistant from two sites (*Conflict Lines*), and `Bento, Ferreira, & Hall (2018, p. 355)` show the Voronoi vertex of 3 sites is the circumcenter where $d(O,A)=d(O,B)=d(O,C)$. `Olson (1975, Act 16, p. 9 / PDF p. 15)` demonstrates via paper folding that creasing two perpendicular bisectors yields point $N$ where $AN = BN = CN$.

### (c) Multi-Step Congruence-to-Isosceles Proof Chaining, Overlapped Figures & `ASS`/`SSA` Prevention
- **Specific Proof Errors in Congruence (`Scristia et al., 2022`)**:
  - Analyzing `N = 25` Grade 9 written congruence proofs (`pp. 3162–3165`), `Scristia et al. (2022)` identify exact errors mirrored in our Slice 5 exercises: **(1)** confusing perpendicular ($\perp$) with parallel ($\parallel$) notation and misreading which segments are given perpendicular (`AR`, `AS`, `p. 3163` — directly relevant to `Ex. #11` $HO \perp OB, DB \perp OB$); **(2)** claiming equal angles without valid reason (`AY`, `p. 3163`); and **(3)** completing true steps only to conclude **`Angle-Side-Side Postulate` (`ASS`/`SSA`)**, unaware that `ASS` is invalid (`KR`, `p. 3164` — the exact trap on `Ex. #2`!).
- **Overlapped Triangle Visual Burden & Tracing Relief (`Biber, 2020`; `Yeo & Tzeng, 2019`; `Miyazaki et al., 2019`)**:
  - `Biber (2020, Table 2, p. 1151)` found that Grade 9 accuracy across similarity/congruence configurations dropped sharply when triangles were **overlapped (`44%–67%`)** compared with **adjacent (`71%–95%`)**, noting that separating overlapped triangles helps learners spot shared parts (`p. 1154`). All three Slice 5 homework problems (`#2`, `#8`, `#11`) feature **overlapped triangles** ($\triangle PQS$ & $\triangle PRT$; $\triangle MBC$ & $\triangle NCB$; $\triangle HOE$ & $\triangle DBP$).
  - `Yeo & Tzeng (2019, pp. 1, 7–8)` demonstrate that **index-finger tracing** over worked geometry diagrams reduces extraneous cognitive load and improves test accuracy for learners across both low and high working-memory spans. Wave 2/3 worksheets should instruct students to trace each target triangle perimeter before writing congruence triplets.
  - `Miyazaki et al. (2019, pp. 3–8)` highlight pairing *planning* and *constructing* proofs with structured **`Looking Back` (`EIA`)** — having students highlight theorem numbers/warrants (`①–⑤`) to ensure every deductive step is explicitly backed (`p. 7`).
