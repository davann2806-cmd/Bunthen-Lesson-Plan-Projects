## 1. Textbook Ground-Truth Audit & Pedagogical Clarifications (`Book p. 5 & p. 11`)

Visual inspection of `pdf_p10.png` (`Book p. 5`) and `pdf_p16.png` (`Book p. 11`) establishes exact verbatim textbook statements and four crucial pedagogical clarifications required in `LS_Plan_RAT23`, `Teaching_Scenario_RAT23`, `Teaching_Guideline_RAT23`, and `Printable_Materials_RAT23`:

### 1.1 Verbatim Properties (`2.3. លក្ខណៈ`, `Book p. 5`)
1. **Property 1 (Cross-Product Equivalence)**: `1. តាង \frac{a}{b} និង \frac{c}{d} ជាពីរចំនួនសនិទាន នោះ \frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} លុះត្រាតែ ad = bc ។`
   - *Pedagogical Clarification #1*: Make the implicit non-zero denominator condition ($b \neq 0, d \neq 0$) explicit on the board because `Yetim & Alkan (2013)` document that `31.5%` of middle-schoolers treat denominator `0` as "having no effect".
2. **Property 2 (Scaling & Simplification)**: `2. តាង \frac{a}{b} ជាចំនួនសនិទាននិង n ជាចំនួនគត់រ៉ឺឡាទីប n \neq 0 នោះ \frac{a}{b} = \frac{na}{nb} ។` *(Add standard Khmer word space `សនិទាន និង`.)*
   - *Pedagogical Clarification #2*: Highlight bidirectional use — left-to-right **expansion (`ពង្រីកប្រភាគ`)** and right-to-left **cancellation/simplification (`សម្រួលប្រភាគ`)**, including $n = -1 \implies \dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{-a}{-b}$ (which directly explains Worked Example `ខ` and Exercise `1`!).
3. **Property 3 (Three Equivalent Minus-Sign Positions)**: `3. តាង \frac{a}{b} ជាចំនួនសនិទាន នោះ -\frac{a}{b} = \frac{-a}{b} = \frac{a}{-b} ។`
   - *Pedagogical Clarification #3*: Contrast single minus sign ($-\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{-a}{b} = \dfrac{a}{-b}$) against double minus sign ($\dfrac{-a}{-b} = +\dfrac{a}{b}$ and $-\dfrac{-a}{b} = +\dfrac{a}{b}$) vs. triple minus sign ($-\dfrac{-a}{-b} = -\dfrac{a}{b}$) so students do not distribute a front minus sign to both numerator and denominator (`-\frac{a}{b} \neq \frac{-a}{-b}`).
4. **Property 4 (Cross-Product Inequality under Positive Denominators)**: `4. ចំពោះចំនួនសនិទាន \frac{a}{b} និង \frac{c}{d} ដែល b > 0 និង d > 0 បើ \frac{a}{b} < \frac{c}{d} នោះ ad < bc ; បើ ad < bc នោះ \frac{a}{b} < \frac{c}{d} ។`
   - *Pedagogical Clarification #4*: Textbook p. 5 includes **no worked example** for Property 4! Yet `Çetin (2022)` proves that `25%` of learners cross-multiply inequalities blindly without checking denominator signs (`0/108` solved rational inequalities with valid steps initially). We embed **POE Trap 2B** ($\dfrac{1}{-2}$ vs. $\dfrac{1}{3}$: naive $1\times 3 = 3 > (-2)\times 1 = -2$ vs. true $-\dfrac{1}{2} < \dfrac{1}{3}$) so students experience firsthand why $b>0, d>0$ is mandatory and why Property `③` must standardize negative denominators before applying Property `④`!

## 3. Synthesis Across the 3 Pedagogical Lenses

### Lens 1 — Student Misconceptions on Rational Properties, Signs & Cross-Multiplication (`Papers 01–06`)
1. **Natural Number Bias (`NNB`) & Bipartite Whole-Number Thinking (`Alibali & Sidney, 2015`; `González-Forte et al., 2021`; `Jarrah et al., 2022`)**:
   - Across `N = 1,262` Grade 5–10 students (including Grade 8), `González-Forte et al. (2021)` identified 8 distinct reasoning profiles (`10.6%` Full NNB, `11.6%` Mult/Div & Fraction NNB, `9.6%` Mult/Div NNB + Blank Fraction, `25.8%` Almost Correct), showing that NNB interferes significantly more in **fractions** (`90.41%` NNB error in Cluster 3) than decimals (`77.74%` correct). `Jarrah et al. (2022)` (`N = 40` Grade 7, CRI analysis) similarly found `> 60%` treated numerators and denominators as two independent whole numbers, with `49%` holding high-confidence misconceptions and `55%` failing part-whole/equivalence transitions. `Alibali & Sidney (2015)` explain that NNB emerges dynamically whenever students' magnitude representation of $\frac{a}{b}$ is weak—causing learners to think $\frac{-15}{36}$ and $\frac{20}{-48}$ cannot be equal because `15 != 20` and `36 != 48`!
2. **Minus-Sign Placement, Double Negatives & Integer-Rational Representation (`Yetim & Alkan, 2013`; `Lee & Boyadzhiev, 2020`)**:
   - `Yetim & Alkan (2013)` (`N = 73` Grade 7, `n = 25` interviewed) found **`72.6%`** operational errors (`16.4%` correct) on integer-rational relationships (`Q3`), **`80.8%`** operational errors (`13.7%` correct) when writing integers as rational quotients $a = \frac{a}{1} = \frac{-a}{-1}$ (`Q7`), **`31.5%`** zero-denominator misconceptions (`Q5`), and widespread inability to divide signs of two negative integers (`p. 218`)—directly predicting student errors on Textbook `លំហាត់ ១` (`p. 11`, seven signed forms of $\pm 5$) and Worked Example `ខ` ($\frac{-25}{-35}$)!
   - `Lee & Boyadzhiev (2020)` (`N = 22`, `41` problems, `47%` overall accuracy) documented confusion between the subtraction/minus sign in front of a fraction ($-\frac{a}{b}$) and signed numerator/denominator ($\frac{-a}{b}, \frac{a}{-b}$), false cross-multiplication in non-proportion contexts, failure to simplify before computing, and confusing `LCD` with `GCF`.
3. **The Negative-Denominator Cross-Multiplication Inequality Trap (`Çetin, 2022`)**:
   - `Çetin (2022)` (`N = 108` across 3 cohorts) demonstrated that **`0/108` (`0%`)** initially solved rational inequalities with completely valid steps: `41%` omitted sign analysis, **`25%` performed blind cross-multiplication (`cross multiply`)**, `11%` ignored the denominator, and students routinely multiplied/divided by negative expressions without reversing `<` to `>` (`pp. 4, 10, 12–13`). This directly explains why Textbook Property 4 (`p. 5`) mandates **`b > 0 និង d > 0`**!

### Lens 2 — Hands-On Equivalence Strips, Number-Line Mirroring & Cambodian Classroom PCK (`Papers 07–10`)
1. **Cambodian Rational-Number PCK Context (`Van et al., 2018`)**:
   - `Van et al. (2018)` studied `N = 54` Cambodian teacher trainers (`n = 33` paired pre/post, `97` classroom observations). Active manipulative + diagnostic questioning raised total scores from `M = 27.3` to `M = 33.2` (`p < .001`) and misconception-diagnosis scores from `5.6` to `8.0` (`p < .001`), yet even post-training only `~25%` could deeply explain *why* students committed rational-number errors (`p. 207`). Embedding explicit misconception prompts and verbatim teacher scripts directly supports Cambodian lower-secondary classrooms.
2. **Bridging Part-Whole Strips to Number-Line Measurement (`Ertuna & Toluk Uçar, 2021`; `Tucker, 2014`; `Barrera-Mora & Reyes-Rodriguez, 2019`)**:
   - `Ertuna & Toluk Uçar (2021)` (`N = 1,111`, Grades 4–7) proved that students link equivalent fractions far better in regional part-whole models (`Mean Rank = 620.85`) than on **number lines** (`Mean Rank = 285.65`, `z = -21.562, p < .001, r = 0.457`), and score far higher on simple fractions (`598.92`) than unreduced equivalent fractions (`167.61`, `z = -26.868, p < .001`). Combining `Tucker's (2014)` concrete-pictorial-symbolic equivalence strip (`REFractions`) with a **1:1 Number-Line Mirror Mat** (`[-1, +1]` where $1\text{ unit} = 6.00\text{ cm}$) lets students physically see both $\frac{-25}{-35} = \frac{5}{7}$ AND why $-\frac{2}{3}, \frac{-2}{3}, \frac{2}{-3}$ all land at $-4.00\text{ cm}$ while $\frac{-2}{-3} = +\frac{2}{3}$ reflects across $0$ to $+4.00\text{ cm}$, grounded in a rural river-level gauge context (`Barrera-Mora & Reyes-Rodriguez, 2019`, `N = 24`).

### Lens 3 — Worked-Example Sequencing, Finger-Tracing & Hub-and-Spoke Structure (`Papers 11–14`)
1. **Correct-First Worked Examples + Self-Explanation (`Barbieri et al., 2022`; `Wesenberg et al., 2022`)**:
   - `Barbieri et al. (2022)` (`N = 230` analyzed) showed that worked-example-supplemented rational-number instruction with self-explanation prompts significantly improved fraction number-line estimation (`F(1,220) = 4.796, p = .030, d = .218`) and buffered low-prior-knowledge learners on conceptual understanding (`b = .331` vs. `.499, p < .001`).
   - `Wesenberg et al. (2022)` (`N = 83`) showed that presenting Erroneous Examples (`EE`) before Correct Examples (`CE`) increased mental load (`\beta = 0.46, p = .035`) and depressed delayed problem-solving (`\beta = -0.47, p < .001`). Therefore, **E4** models the **Correct 3-Step Detective Card (`CE`) first**, followed by **E5** error diagnosis (`EE`) and 4-foil exit checks!
2. **Diagonal Finger-Tracing (`[tracing]`) & Hub-and-Spoke Warrant Bank (`Yeo & Tzeng, 2019`; `Xu et al., 2026`)**:
   - `Yeo & Tzeng (2019)` (`N = 72`, ages 11–13) proved finger-tracing on worked examples significantly improves transfer (`F(3,66) = 6.01, p = .001, \eta^2 = .215`). Students physically trace the two cross-product diagonals (`[ចង្អុលអូសខ្វែង ↘ ad និង ↗ bc]`) and circle negative signs in pure B&W.
   - `Xu et al. (2026)` (`n = 61` vs. `n = 60`) showed that organizing properties into a **Hub-and-Spoke** structure around a single core concept (equivalence of rational quotients $\frac{a}{b}$) reduces cognitive burden compared to memorizing four disconnected formulas.
