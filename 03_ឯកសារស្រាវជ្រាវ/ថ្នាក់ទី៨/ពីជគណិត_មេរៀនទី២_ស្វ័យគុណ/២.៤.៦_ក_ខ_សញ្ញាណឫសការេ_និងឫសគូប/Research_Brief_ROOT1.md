# Research Brief: Grade 8 Algebra — Lesson 2, Section 4.6 (ក & ខ) Square Roots & Cube Roots (`ROOT1`)

## 1. Curriculum & Classroom Scope
- **Textbook**: MoEYS Grade 8 Mathematics, Lesson 2 (`ស្វ័យគុណ`), Section `4.6. ឫសការេ និងឫសគូបនៃចំនួនសនិទាន — ក. ឫសការេ & ខ. ឫសគូប` (pp. 22–24 top) & Exercises `#12, #13, #14, #15` (p. 26).
- **Zero-Tech / Low-Resource Constraint**: Taught **without LCD projector, slides, cut-out manipulatives, or calculators**; anchored in a structured 2-page B&W Student Worksheet (`Worksheet_ROOT1.pdf`) and chalkboard diagrams.
- **Textbook Erratum Guard (p. 22)**: In `គំរូ 1. ក` (`x = 4 ឫស x = -4`), `ឫស` is a typographical error for the conjunction `ឬ` ("or"). Furthermore, for `ខ. x^2 = -9`, never write the undefined real expression $\sqrt{-9}$ as an intermediate equality; state directly that since $x^2 \ge 0$ for every rational/real $x$, $x^2 = -9$ has **no root**.

## 2. International Mathematics Education Evidence Base
1. **Zazkis, R., & Sirotic, N. (2010).** Representing and defining irrational numbers: Exposing the missing link. *Educational Studies in Mathematics*, *74*(1), 1–27. / **Kontorovich, I. (2018).** Why Johnny struggles when encountering square roots. *For the Learning of Mathematics*, *38*(3), 33–37.
   - *Key Finding*: Secondary students persistently confuse the **relational concept** "a square root of $a$" (solutions to $x^2 = a$, yielding $\pm\sqrt{a}$ when $a>0$) with the **single-valued principal radical operator** $\sqrt{a} \ge 0$, routinely writing the false equality $\sqrt{16} = \pm 4$.
   - *Classroom Translation*: Introduce a **Dual-Track Contrast Table** (`សមីការ x^2 = a` vs `និមិត្តសញ្ញា \sqrt{a}`) in `E2 (POE 1)` and codify Warrant `①` vs `②`.
2. **Fischbein, E. (1987).** *Intuition in Science and Mathematics: An Educational Approach*. Reidel. / **Tirosh, D., & Even, R. (1997).** To define or not to define: The case of $(-8)^{1/3}$. *Educational Studies in Mathematics*, *33*(3), 321–336.
   - *Key Finding*: Students overgeneralize either "negatives never have roots" (rejecting $\sqrt[3]{-27} = -3$) or "radicals distribute blindly over negative factors" (attempting $\sqrt{(-64)(-81)} = \sqrt{-64}\cdot\sqrt{-81}$ or claiming no root exists).
   - *Classroom Translation*: Build an **Even-vs-Odd Parity Sign Gate** (`POE 2`) contrasting $x^2 = -9$ (no root) against $x^3 = -27 \implies x = -3$ (unique negative root), and enforce **Radicand-First Evaluation** (Warrant `⑤`: $(-64)(-81) = +64\times 81 > 0 \implies \sqrt{64\times 81} = 8\times 9 = 72$).
