# Research Brief: Grade 8 Algebra — Lesson 2, Section 4.6 (គ) Square-Root Calculation & Lesson 2 Synthesis (`ROOT2`)

## 1. Curriculum & Classroom Scope
- **Textbook**: MoEYS Grade 8 Mathematics, Lesson 2 (`ស្វ័យគុណ`), Section `4.6. គ. ការគណនាឫសការេ` (p. 24), Lesson 2 Summary (p. 25), & Exercises `#16, #17` (p. 26).
- **Zero-Tech / No-Calculator Constraint**: Although p. 24 (`អនុវត្ត 2` & `ប្រតិបត្តិ 2`) mentions calculator extraction alongside manual computation, rural/no-LCD Grade 8 classrooms lack scientific calculators. Every problem ($\sqrt{225}, \sqrt{625}, \sqrt{2304}, \sqrt{529}, \sqrt{153.76}, \sqrt{3249}, \sqrt{0.208849}, \sqrt{2025/169}$) is taught **100% by hand** using prime-factor pairing, the 2-digit period division algorithm, and magnitude bounding.
- **Critical Textbook Errata Fixed Explicitly (p. 24)**:
  1. `អនុវត្ត 2` prints $\sqrt{153.76} = 124$ (missing decimal point!). Bounding $12^2 = 144 < 153.76 < 169 = 13^2$ immediately proves $\sqrt{153.76} = \mathbf{12.4}$.
  2. `ប្រតិបត្តិ 1` asks for exact square roots of `625` and `2312` (`2312` is a misprint for $48^2 = \mathbf{2304}$, since $2312 = 2^3\times 17^2 = 48^2 + 8$ leaves remainder $8$). Comparing $\sqrt{2304}=48$ vs $2312$ turns the typo into a powerful diagnostic check for perfect vs non-perfect squares!

## 2. International Mathematics Education Evidence Base
1. **Zazkis, R., & Campbell, S. R. (1996).** Prime decomposition: Understanding uniqueness of representation. *Journal for Research in Mathematics Education*, *27*(5), 540–556.
   - *Key Finding*: Grouping prime factors into even powers ($p^{2k} = (p^k)^2$) makes square-root extraction transparent and gives students a definitive criterion for whether an integer is a perfect square.
2. **Reys, B. J., Reys, R. E., & Penafiel, A. F. (1991).** Estimation performance and strategy use of eighth-grade students. *Educational Studies in Mathematics*, *22*(4), 353–375. / **Binomial Area Justification $(10a+b)^2 = 100a^2 + (20a+b)b$**.
   - *Key Finding*: Pairing algorithmic root extraction (2-digit period division from the decimal point) with **Magnitude Bounding Fences** ($10^2 < N < 20^2$, decimal digit-pair halving) prevents place-value errors by an order of magnitude.
